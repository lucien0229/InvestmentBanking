### 1.2 Evidence labels

- **Verified fact** — directly observed on the source-owning provider's official page, help center, terms, privacy page, trust center, or product surface.
- **Evidence-backed inference** — a bounded implication supported by one or more verified mechanisms; the provider did not claim this implication for the proposed product.
- **Product design decision** — a choice Ticket 11 may adopt. Nothing in this source-notes asset turns a candidate implication into a final decision.
- **Channel hypothesis** — a proposed acquisition route whose ability to generate qualified Individual Banker traffic or purchases remains unvalidated.
- **Unresolved validation question** — requires this product's own instrumented visitor, buyer, activation, retention, refund, or referral evidence.

### 1.3 Interpretation guardrails

- An official page proves what that provider currently exposes; it does not prove that the mechanism raises conversion for this product.
- A first-party customer story is curated and self-selected. It can preserve one named customer's language but cannot prove prevalence, market size, typical time savings, CAC, conversion rate, or willingness to pay.
- A public price proves an available offer, not this product's willingness to pay or price ceiling.
- A privacy or trust statement proves only the stated provider/product/configuration. It cannot be copied into this product's claims without matching implementation, contracts, architecture, and verification.
- Searchable page titles and copy can establish **verified observed vendor language**. They do not establish query volume, ranking difficulty, or the language used by most bankers.
- The official OpenAI Investment Banking plugin v0.1.29 remains a capability baseline only. No source below treats it as a competitor, distribution dependency, or market-size proof.

## 3. Verified Mechanism Findings

The following statements are **Verified facts** about the observed public surfaces, not claims that they will work for the proposed product.

1. Banker-adjacent vendors expose dedicated pages for a role, workflow, deliverable, or control problem rather than relying only on a broad category page [S01, S03, S12, S16, S19].
2. Public proof can include a visible synthetic/template artifact, native-format sample download, UI screenshot/video, recorded workflow education, or time-limited sample workspace before purchase [S03, S05, S09, S12, S19, S32-S34].
3. Finance and M&A products publicly expose direct individual purchase/trial paths alongside optional sales-led enterprise paths [S02, S07-S09, S13, S16-S18].
4. Pricing/terms surfaces can state the exact billing unit, included usage, exceptional charge, warning, payment method, cancellation, invoice, renewal, refund/guarantee clock, and exclusions [S08, S17, S18, S24, S26].
5. Professional software trust surfaces separate security, compliance, privacy, and detailed control evidence; stronger examples expose dated audit/penetration/continuity/vendor-risk materials [S10, S14, S15, S27-S30].
6. “No training” and “provider retention” are separate claims. OpenAI's current API terms distinguish training, abuse-monitoring logs, application state, endpoint eligibility, and approval-dependent ZDR [S27-S28].
7. Product-event alerts can use multiple channels, deep-link to the affected object, and let the user choose, stop, or delete alerts [S21].
8. Artifact sharing can be bound to owner, recipient, role, export, revocation, and plan eligibility; referral rewards can be bound to a clean paid subscription and delayed beyond a refund window [S22-S24].
9. First-party vendors use recorded webinars, newsletters, template libraries, help centers, release notes, and customer stories as adjacent acquisition/trust/activation surfaces [S03-S06, S11, S20, S31].
10. Analytics/retargeting technologies are deployed by at least one professional financial-data provider, but the public privacy page exposes no evidence of incremental conversion or unit economics [S25].

## 4. Evidence-Backed Inferences for the Ticket 11 Decision

These are **Evidence-backed inferences**, not externally verified outcomes and not yet Product design decisions.

### 4.1 Discovery and positioning

- An outcome-first acquisition architecture should lead with the current banker job and failure state—revision control, cross-file inconsistency, evidence inspection, tie-out recovery, readiness—then reveal AI as part of the mechanism. This is consistent with banker-adjacent pages that lead with pitch-book rework, hardcodes, broken links, CIM preparation, document search, and exact deliverables [S01, S16, S19].
- Search/content pages should be organized by trigger/workflow/deliverable/control question, not as mass programmatic variants. Official vendors expose a small number of substantive industry, feature, template, and use-case pages with visible product proof [S01, S03, S12, S19].
- Public customer language may improve comprehension only after qualification: FirmRoom's “online research on the top VDR providers” statement is a single curated anecdote, so it can justify observing directory/comparison intent but cannot prioritize review directories as a launch loop [S11].

### 4.2 Proof and trust

- The strongest pre-checkout proof ladder for this product is likely: public synthetic artifact and annotated mechanism → no-signup synthetic interactive workspace → recorded exact control loop → methodology/QC/readiness explanation → pricing and preflight preview. The sources show each component can exist publicly; none proves the combined sequence's conversion effect [S03, S05, S09, S12, S19, S32-S34].
- A public proof asset should show the failure and recovery, not merely a polished end state. Template/sample pages prove an artifact can be inspected; DocSend and FirmRoom prove workflow/product controls can be shown visually; the proposed product must add exact source-to-output lineage, history, deterministic failure/recovery, and readiness consequences [S03, S09, S12, S19].
- Trust should be a progressive evidence system: concise checkout answers, a detailed Trust/Security/Privacy page, provider-specific retention matrix, deletion/export behavior, public status/release history, and deeper audit/vendor-risk documents when actually available [S06, S10, S14-S15, S27-S30].
- “No shared-model training” can be promised only after the selected provider/product/configuration is verified. It must not be used as shorthand for zero provider retention, no support access, or zero product storage [S27-S29].

### 4.3 Purchase, activation, retention, and expansion

- A direct checkout can support a professional product if it answers product scope, billing, included capacity, exceptional charge, refund/guarantee, invoice, cancellation, and compatibility questions before payment [S02, S08, S13, S17-S18, S24, S26].
- The activation path should be a short ordered sequence with compatibility/preflight, explicit next action, resumable state, and self-service recovery/help. Public finance products expose ordered setup, install/help resources, and trial-to-paid notices rather than requiring a human for every account [S04, S07, S17].
- Retention should be driven by a changed business object and route the banker back to the exact affected state. Koyfin's document/event alerts support the mechanism of event → chosen channel → deep link → user-controlled frequency, but not this product's cadence [S21].
- Private artifact sharing can create a future Team signal without inserting marketing into a confidential client deliverable. The recipient experience should remain revision-specific, role-bound, revocable, and authority-aware [S23].
- Referral credit should be account-level, optional, and paid only after a clean paid conversion survives the guarantee/refund period; it should never depend on sharing Deal Materials [S22, S24].

### 4.4 Channel mechanics

- High-intent organic search plus workflow/problem pages is an evidence-backed mechanism candidate because banker-adjacent vendors maintain substantive role/workflow/deliverable pages. Its ability to produce qualified visits for this product remains unvalidated [S01, S03, S12, S16, S19].
- Public synthetic proof plus a no-signup interactive workspace is an evidence-backed conversion mechanism candidate; its completion-to-pricing and pricing-to-purchase rates are unresolved [S19, S32-S34].
- Recorded education and partner/creator distribution are observable mechanisms, but audience fit, editorial independence, founder effort, and clean paid conversion remain unknown [S05, S31].
- Review directories are at most a later trust/discovery supplement until authentic users and review volume exist. Vendor-displayed badges/testimonials do not prove directory attribution or independent trust [S01, S11, S20].
- Paid search and retargeting are technically observable and trackable but remain **Channel hypotheses**. Cookie deployment is not acquisition-performance evidence [S25].
- LinkedIn organic, finance/IB communities, Reddit, X, newsletters/podcasts outside the observed first-party surfaces, software directories, and creator partnerships have no current conversion evidence in this research. Unavailable social backends were not bypassed.
- The OpenAI/Codex/plugin ecosystem has no verified acquisition mechanism in this research and must not be assumed as a distribution dependency.

## 9. Explicit Channel Hypotheses

The following are **Channel hypotheses** for Ticket 11 to accept, reject, or stage. None is a verified demand fact.

1. A small cluster of high-intent workflow/problem pages plus synthetic proof will outperform a broad “AI for investment banking” home page on qualified-proof completion.
2. A no-signup synthetic Deal Workspace that demonstrates conflict detection, Banker correction, deterministic recovery, lineage, and Package Readiness will produce more pricing views than screenshot-only proof.
3. Search visitors triggered by CIM refresh, cross-file inconsistency, senior-review tie-out, or new-source revision will be more qualified than visitors seeking generic free templates.
4. Recorded exact-mechanism walkthroughs will reduce trust and comprehension abandonment among visitors unwilling to upload real materials before payment.
5. Retargeting only visitors who completed meaningful synthetic proof may be more efficient and less trust-damaging than broad finance-interest retargeting.
6. A referral credit issued only after a clean paid purchase survives the guarantee window will reduce abuse while preserving a professional referral loop.
7. Private exact-Revision sharing with an invited colleague will be a stronger future Team expansion signal than a generic “invite your team” prompt.

## 11. Research Verdict for the Main Ticket 11 Agent

**Evidence-backed conclusion:** the primary sources support designing acquisition and conversion as one connected product system, not as a list of promotion channels:

`specific trigger/workflow page → inspectable synthetic outcome → stateful sample control loop → precise trust evidence → transparent pricing/preflight preview → direct checkout → guided first correct use → event-driven Deal return → private artifact/referral/team signal`.

What is verified is the availability of the component mechanisms across current professional/finance/M&A software. What remains unverified is their traffic, conversion, CAC, willingness-to-pay, activation, retention, and referral performance for this product. Ticket 11 should therefore choose and instrument a narrow launch sequence while keeping all numerical targets explicitly labeled **Product design decisions** or **initial instrumentation targets**, never industry benchmarks.
