# Initial ICP Workflow and Self-Serve Purchase Context

**Research scope:** United States-first Initial Design ICP — execution-oriented Individual Bankers at overseas boutique investment banks, small or midsize M&A advisory firms, and independent transaction advisory practices  
**Research date / access date for all web sources unless otherwise stated:** 2026-07-31  
**Decision boundary:** This asset describes the ICP and its operating and purchase context. It does **not** select a first workflow, set a price, specify the product, or reconsider whether the product will be built.

## 1. Reading rules and premises preserved

Every material statement below is labeled:

- **Verified fact** — directly supported by a cited public source.
- **Evidence-backed inference** — a bounded conclusion from multiple cited facts; it is not directly measured.
- **Unvalidated design hypothesis** — a proposition the product must test through self-serve behavior and product telemetry, without interviews, manual pilots, founder sales, banker advisors, or high-touch service.

The confirmed premises remain unchanged:

- The product is a persistent **Deal Workspace**, not a one-shot assistant.
- The official Investment Banking plugin is the minimum capability baseline, not a competitor.
- The Individual-First Release is premium and self-serve. Advanced team collaboration, permissions, and approval workflows are deferred.
- AI performs provider-side execution and checking. The Individual Banker retains material professional judgment and control over external use.
- The operating model excludes founder sales, warm-network dependency, manual pilots, banker advisors, high-touch implementation, and enterprise-procurement dependency.
- Competitors can establish workflow mechanics, price anchors, product expectations, and distribution patterns; they cannot veto the product or force a whitespace search.

## 2. Decision-ready answer

### 2.1 Who the Initial Design ICP is in operational terms

**Evidence-backed inference.** The useful initial segment is not “all investment bankers.” It is an execution-oriented individual who:

1. personally produces or reviews models, marketing materials, diligence work, process trackers, or decision memoranda;
2. works in a small deal team and carries work across multiple stages rather than handing each stage to a separate specialist;
3. has repeated revision pressure from management, senior bankers, clients, counterparties, and external advisers;
4. can both pay for the product through self-serve checkout **and** use it with the intended data under employer, client, confidentiality, and regulatory rules; and
5. values native, reviewable work product more than an untraceable answer.

Current boutique and middle-market job descriptions support points 1–3. Dinan describes analysts/associates working in small teams across models, confidential information memoranda, management presentations, virtual data rooms, buyer lists, weekly client status reports, and multiple engagements, using Excel, PowerPoint, Word, Capital IQ, Salesforce, and web-based deal systems. GLC Advisors and AQ Technology Partners similarly describe small-team, all-phase execution, modeling, presentations, buyer work, research, and multiple projects. These are **role descriptions**, not time studies or proof of software purchase authority. [Dinan, “Associate or Analyst,” undated current page](https://www.dinancompany.com/careers/associate-or-analyst); [GLC Advisors, “Investment Banking Analyst,” current posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/glcadvisors/jobs/5152713007); [GLC Advisors, “Investment Banking Associate,” current posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/glcadvisors/jobs/4962774007); [AQ Technology Partners, “Investment Banking Analyst,” current posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/aqtechnologypartners/jobs/4006683005). All accessed 2026-07-31.

**Critical distinction.** Public evidence establishes that individuals can technically buy some banker-adjacent tools through a web checkout. It does **not** establish that every analyst, associate, vice president, managing director, independent adviser, or overseas-firm employee may expense the purchase or upload confidential deal information. Payment authority is not data-use authority.

### 2.2 What the product context must fit

**Evidence-backed inference.** The job is an event-driven, multi-month sequence of evidence collection, analysis, artifact production, review, circulation, and revision. It is not a daily chat habit and not one linear document-generation task.

The durable unit of value is:

> Preserve the chain from source → evidence → assumption → calculation → artifact → review or decision across repeated changes to the same deal.

That chain explains why the confirmed Deal Workspace premise fits the work. It does not decide which link becomes the first released workflow.

### 2.3 What remains unproven

The research does **not** prove:

- the distribution of time across tasks for this exact ICP;
- which job title usually controls a corporate card, expense threshold, vendor approval, or confidential-data deployment decision;
- willingness to pay, price elasticity, realized contract value, or the best billing metric;
- which workflow creates the strongest activation or retention;
- conversion, repeat-use, refund, or support rates;
- that a public vendor’s security or product claims have been independently verified; or
- that rules applying to a registered broker-dealer, Capital Acquisition Broker, fairness opinion, private placement, or reportable merger apply to every ICP member or every transaction.

These gaps are explicit product risks, not reasons to reopen go/no-go.

## 3. The real end-to-end deal workflow

### 3.1 Authoritative workflow spine

**Verified fact.** FINRA’s Series 79 content outline, revised October 2025, defines critical functions for investment-banking representatives. It covers:

- collecting and analyzing company, industry, market, regulatory, and financial information from filings, commercial and proprietary databases, websites, and client or adviser communications;
- company and industry analysis, financial statement analysis, projections, valuation, capital structure, and transaction alternatives;
- sell-side engagement, valuation, buyer analysis, teaser and confidential offering materials, confidentiality agreements, bidding instructions, management presentations, indications of interest, data rooms, diligence, final bids and letters of intent, definitive agreements, fairness opinions, signing, closing conditions, and transaction communications; and
- buy-side strategy, target and confidential-material review, valuation through comparable companies, precedent transactions, DCF and LBO analysis, bids, financing, diligence, agreements, and closing.

[FINRA, “Series 79 – Investment Banking Representative Exam,” current page](https://www.finra.org/registration-exams-ce/qualification-exams/series79); [FINRA, *Series 79 Content Outline*, revised October 2025](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/Series_79_Content_Outline.pdf). Accessed 2026-07-31.

This is an authoritative registered-representative capability outline. It is not a claim that every Initial ICP member is registered, performs every task personally, or uses the same process.

| 3. It describes management draft iterations, a buyer universe, teasers, NDAs, a confidential memorandum, IOIs, management presentations, LOIs, due diligence, purchase agreement work, consents, and closing. This is one firm’s process guide, not a market-wide duration benchmark. Accessed 2026-07-31.

**Verified fact.** Capstone Partners describes transaction execution from diligence and valuation through marketing, buyer engagement, management presentations, negotiation, and definitive documents. Lincoln International describes analysts as the analytical anchors responsible for the accuracy of modeling and deliverables across a transaction. [Capstone Partners, “Bassem Basta,” undated current page](https://www.capstonepartners.com/advisors/bassem-basta/); [Lincoln International, “Campus & Early Careers,” undated current page](https://www.lincolninternational.com/campus-early-careers/). Accessed 2026-07-31.

### 3.3 Buy-side mirror

**Verified fact.** The October 2025 FINRA Series 79 outline includes a buy-side sequence: assess acquisition strategy and capacity; identify/review the target and confidential materials under an NDA; analyze the target; perform preliminary or pro forma valuation using comps, precedent transactions, DCF, or LBO methods; submit preliminary bids; arrange financing; conduct follow-up diligence and requests; submit a final bid; negotiate agreements; and close. [FINRA, *Series 79 Content Outline*, revised October 2025](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/Series_79_Content_Outline.pdf). Accessed 2026-07-31.

**Evidence-backed inference.** The Deal Workspace model should support both sides’ common evidence and artifact primitives while retaining side-specific permissions, objectives, and decision records. The public evidence base is more sell-side-heavy, so no claim is made that sell-side frequency or pain rankings automatically transfer to buy-side work.

## 4. Triggers, cadence, and repeat-use conditions

### 4.1 Trigger map

**Evidence-backed inference.** Work recurs when the deal state changes, especially when:

- a mandate, target, offer, or kickoff starts a workspace;
- a new management file, monthly actual, forecast, customer schedule, filing, or market-data refresh arrives;
- an NDA is signed or VDR access changes;
- a senior banker, client, management team, buyer, board member, committee, or adviser comments on a draft;
- an IOI, bid, LOI, meeting, diligence response, agreement draft, or closing condition is due;
- a diligence finding changes EBITDA, working capital, debt-like items, valuation, risk, or transaction terms; or
- the deal closes, dies, pauses, restarts, or becomes the basis for another mandate.

The public merger history for TaskUs shows repeated offers, special-committee and adviser meetings, an NDA, an initial diligence request, electronic data-room access, management forecasts and quarterly results, repeated valuation and fairness materials, and drafts of the merger agreement and disclosure letter. It proves repeated state changes in one documented public-company transaction; it does not estimate typical lower-middle-market frequency. [TaskUs, definitive proxy statement, filed 2025-08-08](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1829864/000119312525176997/d111106ddefm14a.htm). Accessed 2026-07-31.

### 4.2 Repeat-use conditions

**Evidence-backed inference.** Repeat use is plausible when the workspace makes an update cheaper and safer than reconstructing context manually:

- sources, periods, currencies, units, definitions, and access rights remain controlled;
- new evidence can be reconciled against the prior version;
- an assumption or calculation change identifies affected downstream artifacts;
- Excel, PowerPoint, Word, PDF, tracker, memo, and process records remain linked but editable;
- open questions, decisions, approvals, and unresolved conflicts persist;
- a banker can review, accept, revise, reject, export, or circulate — never merely trust a generated output; and
- the next deal can reuse process structure without carrying confidential facts across workspaces.

**Unvalidated design hypothesis.** Within-deal retention will be driven more by successful response to a second and third state change than by the first generated artifact. Across-deal retention will depend on mandate frequency, portable process memory, and fast clean-room startup for the next Deal Workspace.

## 5. Work that is slow, risky, or quality-critical

Public evidence does not provide a representative time-allocation study for the exact ICP. The following ranking is therefore a **value map**, not a first-workflow ranking.

| Work cluster | Why time expands | Why quality matters | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source collection, normalization, and evidence control | Data arrives in inconsistent Excel files, PDFs, accounting exports, emails, and VDR folders; definitions, periods, units, and versions conflict | Errors propagate into models, materials, bids, and advice; missing support creates rework and weakens review | FINRA requires collection and analysis across public, commercial, proprietary, and client sources; current job descriptions and firm process pages show repeated organization and synthesis |
| Forecasting, valuation, QoE, working capital, and transaction models | Calculations are interconnected; market, operating, financing, and transaction assumptions change | Results affect price, financing, terms, bids, fairness work, and recommendations | FINRA Series 79; Axial failure sample; CLA QoE synthesis |
| Client, buyer, board, and committee materials | Narrative, numbers, charts, footnotes, sources, and disclosure must remain consistent through many review rounds | Unsupported or inconsistent claims damage credibility and may alter decisions or require withdrawal/rework | FINRA Series 79; role and firm descriptions; FINRA fairness-opinion procedures where applicable |
| Process, diligence, Q&A, and deadline control | Many parties, requests, permissions, dependencies, and versions move simultaneously | Delay can extend diligence, create fatigue, miss a bid/filing/closing gate, or disclose material to the wrong party | Stout process; FINRA private-placement diligence notices; conditional FTC waiting-period rules |
| Quality control, supervisory evidence, and recordkeeping | Review must tie numbers and claims back to sources and preserve decisions/correspondence where applicable | Missing evidence, approval, or records can cause professional, client, contractual, supervisory, or enforcement consequences | FINRA Rules 3110, 4511, 5150; FINRA Notices 10-22, 23-08, 24-09, 21-29; SEC enforcement |

### Failure and consequence evidence

**Verified fact, bounded sample.** Axial analyzed 75 unsuccessful transactions sourced through its network in 2025. It attributed 25.3% to non-QoE diligence findings, 21.3% to QoE EBITDA discrepancies, 14.7% to renegotiation, 13.3% to seller decisions, 10.7% to financing, and 8.0% to business underperformance. This is a platform-owned failure sample, not a census of U.S. M&A or proof that software would have prevented the failures. [Axial, “Dead Deal Report: Unpacking 2025’s Broken LOIs,” updated 2026-01-27](https://www.axial.net/forum/dead-deal-report-unpacking-2025s-broken-lois/). Accessed 2026-07-31.

**Verified fact, secondary professional synthesis.** CLA states that sell-side QoE work focuses on normalized EBITDA, working capital, aggressive or undocumented add-backs, and data readiness; it describes standard diligence as often taking six to eight weeks and warns that delays can create deal fatigue, leakage, or underperformance. Treat its “around one in three signed LOIs fail” statement as a professional synthesis rather than an independent population estimate. [CLA, “How a Sell-Side Quality of Earnings Can Help Close a Business Sale,” published 2026-06-01](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/blogs/private-equity/how-a-sellside-quality-of-earnings-can-help-close-a-business-sale). Accessed 2026-07-31.

**Verified fact, conditional regulatory context.**

- FINRA Notice 23-08 says private-placement investigations should use primary documents, critically analyze third-party reports, follow red flags, document inquiries/research/analysis, and allow sufficient time. It also states that an associated person has an independent obligation in applicable circumstances. [FINRA Regulatory Notice 23-08, published 2023-05-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/23-08).
- FINRA Notice 10-22 states that a firm cannot blindly rely on an issuer, management, or an auditor when conducting the reasonable investigation required in applicable Regulation D offerings. [FINRA Regulatory Notice 10-22, published 2010-04-20](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/10-22).
- FINRA Rule 3110 requires a supervisory system and written evidence of principal review of applicable investment-banking transactions. FINRA Rule 4511 requires members to make and preserve books and records; FINRA Rule 5150 requires written fairness-opinion procedures and specified reviews and disclosures. [FINRA Rule 3110](https://www.finra.org/finramanual/rules/r3110/); [FINRA Rule 4511](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4511); [FINRA Rule 5150](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5150). Current rules accessed 2026-07-31.
- The SEC announced $392.75 million in combined civil penalties against 26 firms for widespread electronic-communication recordkeeping failures. This demonstrates the stakes for regulated firms; it does not make every product interaction a regulated record. [SEC, “Twenty-Six Firms to Pay More Than $390 Million Combined to Settle SEC’s Charges for Widespread Recordkeeping Failures,” published 2024-08-14](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-98).
- For a transaction subject to the HSR Act, parties generally cannot close until the applicable waiting period expires or is terminated. This is a conditional closing dependency, not a feature requirement for every lower-middle-market deal. [FTC, “Premerger Notification and the Merger Review Process,” current page](https://www.ftc.gov/advice-guidance/competition-guidance/guide-antitrust-laws/mergers/premerger-notification-merger-review-process). Accessed 2026-07-31.

**Evidence-backed inference.** The product must treat “fast” as subordinate to reproducibility, evidence completeness, confidentiality, version control, and human approval. A faster unsupported answer can increase, not reduce, total deal work.

| Source class | Examples and controls required |

| Format/system | Required artifact families |
|---|---|
| Excel | Historical spreading and normalization; forecast/three-statement model; comps, precedents, DCF, LBO, merger or transaction analyses; QoE, working capital and debt-like items; buyer/bid matrices; source, request, Q&A, condition and process trackers |

**Evidence-backed inference.** Banker-native output is necessary but insufficient. The product must preserve editable Office round trips, reader-ready PDFs, and the evidence and calculation spine that makes each output reviewable.

### 6.3 Handoff topology

The common handoffs are:

1. management/CFO/client → banker execution team;
2. analyst/associate → VP/MD/principal review;
3. banker ↔ client management and board/committee;
4. banker ↔ buyer or investor;
5. banker ↔ legal, accounting, tax, IT, insurance, market, and financing advisers;
6. model/source owners → memo/deck/tracker owners;
7. active deal team → compliance/records/archive; and
8. prior version → updated source/model/artifact after every material event.

**Evidence-backed inference.** The Individual-First Release can defer multi-user permissions and approvals without pretending these handoffs do not exist. It should make the individual’s outgoing package, evidence, unresolved items, and required human control explicit; it should not simulate absent approvals.

### 7.2 What can and cannot be inferred

**Evidence-backed inference.**

- Public checkout, card payment, no-card trials, transparent limits, and in-product billing prove that premium self-serve mechanics exist for adjacent professional tools.
- The strongest purchase fit is likely an owner/principal/independent adviser or an employee operating inside a firm’s approved small-purchase and vendor policy.
- An employee may be able to pay yet still lack authority to install software, connect licensed data, upload client materials, or use an AI provider. Employer and client controls remain binding.
- The statutory M&A-broker exemption for specified eligible privately held company transactions defines a legal category; it does not establish purchase or confidential-data authority. [15 U.S.C. § 78o(b)(13), current through 2026-07-16](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A15+section%3A78o+edition%3Aprelim%29). Accessed 2026-07-31.

**Unvalidated design hypothesis.** The product should qualify the self-serve ICP behaviorally at activation: “I am authorized to purchase this software and use these materials,” with a public-data or sanitized sample path for evaluators who are not yet authorized. This is a product gate, not a substitute for firm policy or legal permission.

## 8. Premium value and pricing evidence

### 8.1 Evidence that supports premium value

**Evidence-backed inference.** Premium value is plausible when the product protects or compresses work that:

- affects price, financing, terms, committee or client advice;
- must stay consistent across multiple banker-native artifacts;
- recurs across a long, deadline-driven process;
- requires evidence, calculation, review, and version records; and
- otherwise consumes skilled individual execution and senior review time.

The workflow evidence, Axial failure sample, conditional regulatory obligations, and current adjacent price points support this value mechanism. They do not measure demand or set price.

### 8.2 Pricing conclusion allowed by the evidence

**Verified price anchors.**

| Product pattern | Current public price observed | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Macabacus individual productivity | $200/year Basic; $360/year Professional | Individual, self-serve annual purchase for banker-relevant Office tooling | WTP for a governed Deal Workspace |
| FirmRoom self-serve VDR | $395/$695/$995 per month | Premium card checkout for transaction-specific storage, control, and audit mechanics | Individual employee authority or equivalent value for this product |
| Equidam outcome package | $412.25 one-off | Self-serve purchase of a professional valuation artifact package | Investment-banking workflow demand |
| Microsoft 365 Copilot Business | $18/user/month annual; $25.20 monthly commitment | General AI productivity budget anchor | Transaction execution value |

**Decision.** These sources justify carrying a **premium self-serve** premise into later pricing work. They do not justify a launch price, subscription period, seat/deal/workspace/usage metric, free-trial length, or refund policy. Those remain for the dedicated monetization ticket.

## 9. Trust proof required without demos, implementation, or human advisers

### 9.1 Minimum public proof surface

**Evidence-backed inference.** A no-sales purchase requires the product itself to answer:

1. **Can it handle my files?** Exact compatibility matrix and a preflight for Excel, PowerPoint, Word, PDF, scans/OCR, linked workbooks, macros, fonts/templates, file size, encryption, and unsupported content.
2. **Can I inspect the work?** Sanitized downloadable sample Deal Workspace showing sources, page/cell citations, assumptions, conflicts, calculations, checks, versions, and native Office/PDF outputs.
3. **Will it preserve control?** Explicit material-decision gates, review/accept/reject actions, external-use confirmation, and no claim that AI replaces professional judgment.
4. **What happens to my data?** Accurate, product-specific disclosures for training use, retention, deletion, subprocessors, location, encryption, access, incident response, export, and account closure.
5. **Can I rely on the result operationally?** Visible source freshness, deterministic calculation record, cross-artifact tie-outs, unresolved-item log, job status/recovery, audit trail, and export/delete test.
6. **What do I pay and what happens if it fails?** Public price, included usage, limits/overages, trial or sample boundary, cancellation/refund terms, support scope, and response expectations.

FINRA’s current AI notice states that existing obligations still apply when member firms use generative AI and highlights governance, privacy, integrity, reliability, and accuracy. Its outsourcing notice states that use of a vendor does not relieve a member firm of applicable obligations and identifies confidentiality, data ownership/disposition, incidents, subcontractors, record access, and accuracy as relevant controls. These sources apply to member firms and do not prescribe a universal product checklist; they support the need for explicit provider evidence. [FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09, published 2024-06-27](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-09); [FINRA Regulatory Notice 21-29, published 2021-08-13](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/21-29). Accessed 2026-07-31.

### 9.2 Trust hypotheses to test

**Unvalidated design hypotheses.**

- A small runnable example with inspectable evidence and native outputs will outperform generic AI claims and testimonial-heavy marketing for this ICP.
- Showing the exact calculation, citation, version diff, and unresolved issue will create more trust than showing only a polished answer.
- A permitted public/sanitized-data evaluation path will reduce the gap between technical checkout and employer authorization.
- Independent security evidence will be necessary before confidential live-deal use; the exact certification threshold is not established by this research.

## 10. Activation, repeat use, retention, support, and refund risks

### 10.1 Activation

**Unvalidated design hypothesis.** A valid activation event is:

> The individual imports permitted material into a sample or authorized live Deal Workspace and produces at least one banker-reviewable update with visible evidence, assumptions, checks, and a successful native export, without provider help.

This intentionally does not name the first workflow or hero artifact. “Uploaded a file,” “asked a question,” or “generated text” is too weak because it does not establish professional usability or control.

### 10.2 Repeat-use and retention signals

Candidate behavioral evidence for later instrumentation:

- user returns after a new deal trigger;
- a prior controlled source is reused rather than re-uploaded manually;
- a revised source updates one or more affected calculations/artifacts;
- a banker resolves a conflict, accepts/rejects a result, or records an external-use decision;
- a second Office/PDF/tracker output is produced from the same evidence spine;
- the workspace is reopened during diligence, bidding, agreement, or closing; and
- a clean new workspace is created for the next mandate.

**Retention risks.**

- Deals close, die, pause, or remain episodic; an individual may handle few mandates per year.
- Value can be concentrated in a short diligence or materials burst, encouraging cancellation.
- The individual may lose employer approval, change firms, or be unable to move deal data.
- A workspace that does not preserve usable state across revisions collapses into one-off generation.
- Seat-based recurring pricing may mismatch deal cadence; this is a pricing question, not a conclusion.

### 10.3 Support risks under the Founder Operating Envelope

High-probability asynchronous support cases include:

- damaged, password-protected, scanned, low-quality, or mixed-layout PDFs;
- OCR uncertainty and tables with lost labels or units;
- macro-enabled, externally linked, circular, or protected Excel workbooks;
- PowerPoint/Word template, font, chart, link, pagination, and round-trip fidelity;
- stale, conflicting, missing, licensed, or redistribution-restricted data;
- ambiguous period, currency, units, definitions, transaction side, or version;
- large or slow jobs, partial failure, retries, model cost, limits, and overages;
- permissions, deletion, export, billing, renewal, and account-access problems; and
- users seeking transaction judgment or manual cleanup beyond product support.

**Evidence-backed implication.** The product needs self-diagnostics, resumable jobs, explicit error and recovery states, a compatibility matrix, contextual documentation, sample files, export/delete controls, and a strict line between software support and banker/adviser services. Founder support must not become document cleanup, transaction judgment, or high-touch implementation.

### 10.4 Refund risks

**Risks to design around:**

- a customer pays before discovering an unsupported file or licensed-data constraint;
- output is technically generated but not usable in native Office form;
- the first expensive run fails or exhausts included usage;
- privacy or employer approval blocks live use after purchase;
- a one-off event creates immediate cancellation or refund demand;
- a long trial delays value while a short trial encourages rushed/confidential use;
- unclear renewal, cancellation, overage, or deletion terms create distrust; and
- trial abuse creates material provider cost.

**Evidence-backed inference.** File/data preflight, a sample workspace, transparent output and usage limits, visible job-cost status, clear cancellation/refund terms, and an export/delete test should precede or accompany checkout.

**Unvalidated design hypothesis.** A product-specific “unsupported input or failed usable export” remedy may be more credible than an unrestricted satisfaction promise. No refund policy is selected here. Macabacus’s limited-refund approach is an observed adjacent pattern, not a recommendation.

### 12.3 Limitations

- No user interviews, founder sales, warm-network research, manual pilot, banker adviser, or private customer data was used, consistent with the Founder Operating Envelope.
- No public dataset was found for typical task time allocation, title-level purchase limits, corporate-card policy, confidential-data authority, WTP, activation, retention, support, or refunds for the exact ICP.
- Job postings describe responsibilities and tools, not observed behavior or hours.
- Vendor pages are self-reported; public list price is neither willingness to pay nor realized average selling price.
- Axial evidence reflects its network and source-owned methodology, not the entire U.S. market.
- Public workflow evidence is disproportionately sell-side; buy-side claims are kept closer to the FINRA outline.
- Regulatory statements are conditional on the firm, person, transaction, instrument, and jurisdiction. They are not universalized to overseas firms or unregistered advisers.
- Social and LinkedIn search backends were unavailable in the current Agent Reach environment; no unsupported social evidence was substituted.
- Security certifications for the proposed product were not researched or established.
- This asset does not select the first workflow, prototype a product, set pricing, enter specification, or implement code.
