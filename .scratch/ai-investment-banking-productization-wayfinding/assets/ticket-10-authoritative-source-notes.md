# Ticket 10 Authoritative Source Notes: Premium Self-Serve Pricing, Commercial Terms, and Direct-Cost Inputs

**Research scope:** current primary/first-party evidence for Ticket 10 commercial packaging, price anchors, risk reversal, billing terms, and calculation-ready direct-cost inputs

**Access date:** 2026-08-01

**Geography / release context:** United States, English-first, USD unless a source explicitly states another currency

**Status:** research input; this document does not prove willingness to pay and does not itself choose the Ticket 10 offer

## 1. Purpose and Decision Boundary

This asset records only evidence that can constrain the separate Ticket 10 product decision. It answers narrower questions:

1. How do current professional products expose self-serve price, billing unit, included usage, overage, trial, cancellation, and refund mechanics?
2. Which financial-data and transaction products remain sales-led or transaction-quoted, with no public price that can be used as a verified anchor?
3. Which infrastructure prices can be converted into direct-cost formulas for an Individual Banker account, an active Deal, a processed source page, a model operation, an artifact Revision, and retained archive storage?
4. Which costs remain assumptions because price depends on architecture, negotiated contracts, labor, or an undisclosed sales quote?

The following boundaries are inherited from the repo and are not re-opened here:

- the product will be built;
- the first buyer is an overseas, initially US, Individual Banker able to self-purchase;
- the Individual-First Release must deliver the complete core Controlled Auction Execution Package rather than act as a crippled team lead-generation tier;
- V1 does not buy FactSet, LSEG, S&P Capital IQ, PitchBook, Moody's, Third Bridge, Daloopa, Quartr, Datasite, or similar licensed data for a shared product-owned pool;
- AI execution remains subordinate to evidence, deterministic validation, QC, Banker decisions, and exact-Revision external-use control;
- user-visible pricing must not meter prompts, citations, corrections, reviewer actions, or evidence inspection as the primary value unit.

No public reference price below proves this product's willingness to pay, conversion, active-Deal frequency, or support burden. Those remain live validation questions.

## 2. Research Method and Evidence Classification

### 2.1 Retrieval method

- Agent Reach health check was performed before research by the main Ticket 10 agent. Exa Search had no active backend; no connector was installed or configured.
- Official URLs were discovered with available web search where necessary, then opened and verified against the provider's own pricing, product, documentation, FAQ, or regulator page using the Agent Reach Web/Jina Reader path. A few dynamic pages were additionally checked against their first-party rendered HTML.
- Search snippets, AI summaries, third-party price lists, reseller pages, forum posts, and social posts are not used as final price facts.
- No account was registered, no product was purchased, and no sales quote was requested.
- Post-research `agent-reach check-update` reported installed version **v1.5.0** and already current; no update was performed.

### 2.2 Classification labels

- **Verified fact** — stated on the source-owning provider's current official page.
- **Evidence-backed inference** — a bounded conclusion supported by verified facts but not stated verbatim by the source.
- **Product design implication** — a proposed constraint Ticket 10 may adopt; it is not external fact.
- **Assumption** — a calculation input without direct authoritative confirmation for this product.
- **Unresolved validation question** — requires real product usage, purchase, support, or cohort evidence after launch.

### 4.2 Verified commercial mechanics worth carrying into Ticket 10

**Verified fact F-COM-01.** Koyfin publishes a high-end self-serve Advisor Pro price of $299/month equivalent in the annual billing state and accepts major credit cards. It also provides downloadable invoices, auto-renews, permits cancellation effective at the end of the billing period, preserves paid access through that period, and offers a 30-day satisfaction refund. [S05]

**Verified fact F-COM-02.** FirmRoom sells one data room monthly by storage size rather than by internal/external user count, includes unlimited users on paid plans, warns at 95% storage, charges $150/GB/month overage prorated by day, and offers a 14-day fully unlocked trial with no credit card; the trial alone caps invited users at ten. [S08]

**Verified fact F-COM-03.** Macabacus publicly sells Basic and Professional licenses with direct Buy Now links at $200 and $360 per license/year while reserving enterprise features and high-touch support for request-pricing. [S07]

**Verified fact F-COM-04.** Datasite states that Diligence price is customized for every transaction based on project scope, requirements, and timeline; it does not publish a dollar price. [S10]

**Verified fact F-COM-05.** FactSet explicitly describes its pricing as tailored and directs the visitor to Sales for a personalized proposal; S&P Capital IQ Pro uses a request-demo path without a public price. [S11, S13]

**Verified fact F-COM-06.** Ansarada publishes one-room transaction prices from $479 to $3,069 monthly across the selected 250 MB–5 GB tiers, reduces the monthly equivalent for longer commitments, and allows free preparation until the first external guest is invited or 90 days pass. Full features and unlimited users remain constant while storage and term change. [S26]

**Verified fact F-COM-07.** DealRoom does not publish a numeric platform price, but its official pricing page identifies Deal volume, annual commitment, unlimited users, and no per-document charge as its commercial structure. [S28]

**Evidence-backed inference I-COM-01.** Public evidence supports several mature pricing mechanisms—seat/year, user/month, one transaction workspace/month, storage tier, included output volume, and tailored enterprise quote. It does not select the correct mechanism for this product. The selection must come from the product's repeated Deal work and safety incentives.

**Evidence-backed inference I-COM-02.** A pure self-serve price above ordinary horizontal AI subscriptions is operationally plausible: individual AI power-user plans reach $100–$200/month, Koyfin exposes $209–$349 monthly advisor tiers, FirmRoom exposes $395–$995 monthly data-room tiers, and Ansarada publishes transaction-room prices into the thousands. This is not proof that the Initial Design ICP will purchase Ticket 10's proposed price.

**Product design implication P-COM-01.** Lower-priced horizontal AI, research, document sharing, or Office add-ins should be treated as scope markers, not price ceilings. Their commercial object is materially narrower than a revisioned Controlled Auction Execution Package.

**Product design implication P-COM-02.** Sales-led tools with undisclosed prices must be labeled exactly as such. They may show which organizational capabilities trigger sales involvement—licensed data, SSO, onboarding, custom terms, or support—but cannot contribute a numeric WTP data point.

**Product design implication P-COM-03.** FirmRoom's mechanics show a usable pattern for guardrails: meter a predictable resource boundary, display the threshold and warning before overage, require an explicit plan/overage path, and do not meter each ordinary review action. Ticket 10 should translate that principle to active Deals and unusually large source packets, not blindly copy storage pricing.

## 5. Labor-Value Evidence and Limitation

**Verified fact F-LAB-01.** BLS' broad occupation “Securities, Commodities, and Financial Services Sales Agents” explicitly includes investment bankers and describes M&A analysis/advice. Its May 2024 median was $78,140/year or $37.57/hour; the median for the “Securities, commodity contracts, and other financial investments and related activities” industry was $103,370/year. The lowest 10% earned below $47,080 and the highest 10% above $215,210. [S21]

**Verified fact F-LAB-02.** BLS states that corporate-finance and M&A investment bankers generally receive base salary plus potentially substantial bonuses, and that higher-level bonuses may exceed base salary. [S21]

**Evidence-backed inference I-LAB-01.** The broad BLS median understates or blends the economic value of time for many execution-oriented bankers, while annual pay also does not equal employer-loaded cost or the bank's billable/economic value per hour.

**Product design implication P-LAB-01.** Ticket 10 may use a sensitivity range for loaded hourly value, but it must label the range as an **Assumption** rather than claim a BLS-verified investment-banker loaded hourly rate. The BLS figures provide only a conservative public floor/reference and evidence that occupation/bonus dispersion is large.

**Verified fact F-COST-00.** For GPT-5.6, a request with more than 272K input tokens uses the published long-context rates for the whole request. Reasoning tokens are billed as output even though they are not visible; `max_output_tokens` includes reasoning, visible output, and non-visible formatting tokens. An incomplete response can therefore incur input and reasoning cost without returning a visible answer. Cache writes cost 1.25 times uncached input under the shown rate card, and supported regional processing for models released after 2026-03-05 adds 10%. [S01]

**Verified fact F-COST-01.** Google Document AI charges OCR and parsing by page/count; for PDF one PDF page is one page, for XLSX each tab is one page, and for PPTX each slide is one page. [S15]

**Verified fact F-COST-02.** Google states that failed requests returning 4xx or 5xx are not billed. This does not mean all product-level failures are free: a successfully billed parse followed by a failed downstream model, render, validation, or retry still incurs cost. [S15]

**Verified fact F-COST-03.** Cloudflare R2 bills storage using average daily peak GB over a 30-day period, rounds usage to billing units under its policy, and counts multipart upload operations separately; deletion operations themselves are free. [S16]

**Verified fact F-COST-04.** Stripe's public standard price creates a material revenue-linked cost. With Stripe Billing pay-as-you-go, a domestic recurring subscription has a verified combined base formula of `price × (2.9% + 0.7%) + $0.30`. This yields **$18.30** on $500 and **$180.30** on $5,000 before international/currency, dispute, tax, or refund effects. An international card without FX yields 5.1% plus $0.30; with currency conversion, 6.1% plus $0.30. [S17]

**Verified fact F-COST-05.** Stripe says standard card refunds do not add a new refund fee, but the original payment-processing and currency-conversion fees are not returned. A received dispute costs $15; manually countering costs another $15, which is returned if the dispute is won. [S17]

**Evidence-backed inference I-COST-01.** For a high-ticket plan, payment processing is likely to exceed storage and basic OCR cost by a wide margin. Model use, structured extraction, support time, rendering/validation compute, failed retries, and compliance allocation are the larger uncertain drivers.

**Product design implication P-COST-01.** Ticket 10 should model OCR and parsing separately. Plain OCR at $1.50/1,000 pages is not a proxy for reliable table extraction, financial normalization, spreadsheet calculation, Office rendering, cross-artifact tie-out, or human-controlled QC.

**Product design implication P-COST-02.** Account-wide provider free tiers must not be treated as guaranteed per-customer economics. Base unit economics should use list-price marginal rates; free tiers can be shown only as a launch-scale offset.

## 7. Costs That Remain Explicit Assumptions

No reviewed first-party source establishes one universal per-page, per-Deal, or per-Revision cost for the following. Ticket 10 must model them as explicit low/base/high assumptions and later replace them with measured cohort data.

| Cost | Why no single verified unit price applies | Recommended formula for recomputation |
|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheet calculation and deterministic validation | depends on engine, licensing, CPU/memory time, workbook complexity, external links, formula coverage, and retry policy | `workbook_calculation_minutes × compute_rate + validation_jobs × job_rate` |
| PPTX/DOCX/PDF generation | OOXML/PDF libraries may have open-source, commercial-license, or hosted-render pricing; chosen stack is not decided | `artifact_generation_jobs × avg_compute_cost + commercial_license_allocation` |
| Office-native rendering and visual diff | cost depends on Microsoft/third-party runtime, worker isolation, pages/slides, render resolution, and retries | `rendered_pages_or_slides × render_cost + visual_diff_jobs × diff_cost` |
| Backup and disaster recovery | depends on copied bytes, frequency, version retention, region/provider, restore tests, and minimum retention | `backup_GB_month × storage_rate + backup_ops + restore_test_cost` |
| Application database | records, indexes, vector/search features, IOPS, backups, HA and region are architecture choices | `allocated_database_base + storage + read/write/compute overage` |
| Compute/orchestration | workload topology, concurrency, sandboxing, queues, timeouts, and model/tool latency are undecided | `CPU_seconds × CPU_rate + GB_seconds × memory_rate + job_invocations × request_rate` |
| Security review, penetration tests, audit and compliance tooling | public platform quotes are often sales-led; audit/consulting scope varies | `annual_security_and_compliance_budget / paying_accounts` for contribution analysis |
| Public-data access | public does not mean cost-free or reusable; source-specific API terms, rate limits, and rights apply | `source_calls × source_rate + rights/compliance allocation`; default to zero product-owned licensed data in V1 |
| Customer-provided licensed data | rights remain customer/provider-specific and cannot become a shared pool | exclude provider license cost from V1; include only product processing cost |
| Support | founder does not provide high-touch onboarding, but async support and incident handling still consume time | `support_minutes_per_account / 60 × loaded_support_hourly_cost` |
| Evaluation/QC overhead | golden-set runs, model regressions, deterministic checks, and sampled artifact audits depend on release cadence | `eval_jobs × model/tool cost + reviewer_hours × loaded_cost`, allocated by cohort |
| Failed/retried jobs | failure can occur after billable OCR/model/render work | `successful_direct_cost × retry_multiplier + terminal_failure_credit_cost` |
| Archive export/deletion operations | storage deletion may be cheap, but package assembly, integrity verification, download, and purge proof consume compute/ops | `archive_GB_month + export_jobs × export_cost + deletion_jobs × deletion_cost` |
| Taxes, refunds, disputes, chargebacks | jurisdiction, tax nexus, payment-provider product, dispute outcome, and refund policy determine actual cost | `gross_billings × tax/admin rate + refunds × unrecovered_fee + disputes × expected_dispute_cost` |

**Product design implication P-COST-03.** The direct-cost model should keep four distinct layers:

1. **per-account fixed** — database/auth allocation, monitoring, security/compliance, base email, baseline archive;
2. **per active Deal** — source processing, model runs, calculations, current storage, artifact creation, Revision refresh, evaluation allocation;
3. **revenue-linked** — payment processing, refunds, disputes, taxes/collection administration;
4. **support/time** — asynchronous support, incident investigation, manual exception handling, sampled QC.

Optional future product-owned licensed-data cost must remain outside the V1 baseline and appear as a separate future scenario, never hidden in source-processing cost.

## 8. Calculation Primitives for the Ticket 10 Unit-Economics Model

Define the following measured inputs for each low/base/high scenario:

- `A` = paying accounts;
- `D` = active Deals per paying account/month;
- `P` = processed source pages per active Deal/month;
- `Iu_m`, `Ir_m`, `Iw_m`, `Ov_m`, `Or_m` = uncached input, cached-read, cache-write, visible-output, and reasoning tokens for model `m` per active Deal/month;
- `R` = Controlled Auction Execution Package Revisions per active Deal/month;
- `G` = generated/rendered artifact pages or slides per Revision;
- `S_active` = active storage GB per Deal-month;
- `S_archive` = archived storage GB per account-month;
- `J_retry` = multiplicative retry/failure factor, where 1.00 means no repeated paid work;
- `M_support` = support minutes per paying account/month;
- `Price` = collected subscription/additional-Deal revenue before refunds;
- `RefundRate`, `ChargebackRate`, `InternationalCardShare`, `FXShare` = observed payment cohort variables.

Calculation skeleton:

```text
ModelCost_per_Deal = Σm[(Iu_m / 1,000,000 × input_rate_m)
                         + (Ir_m / 1,000,000 × cached_read_rate_m)
                         + (Iw_m / 1,000,000 × cache_write_rate_m)
                         + ((Ov_m + Or_m) / 1,000,000 × output_rate_m)]

Use the full published long-context rate set for any request above 272K input;
apply regional-processing multiplier where applicable.

DocumentCost_per_Deal = (P / 1,000 × OCR_rate)
                      + (P_layout / 1,000 × layout_rate)
                      + (P_structured / 1,000 × extractor_rate)

ArtifactCost_per_Deal = R × (G × assumed_render_cost_per_page
                           + assumed_generation_compute_per_revision
                           + assumed_validation_cost_per_revision)

StorageCost_per_DealMonth = S_active × storage_rate
ArchiveCost_per_AccountMonth = S_archive × archive_storage_rate + retrieval/export operations

RetryAdjustedVariableCost_per_Deal =
  (ModelCost_per_Deal + DocumentCost_per_Deal + ArtifactCost_per_Deal
   + StorageCost_per_DealMonth + other_variable_cost) × J_retry

PaymentCost = Price × (2.9% payments + 0.7% Billing + international/FX adjustments)
            + $0.30 × successful_charges
            + dispute fees

SupportCost = (M_support / 60) × assumed_loaded_support_hourly_cost

COGS_per_Account = fixed_account_allocation
                 + D × RetryAdjustedVariableCost_per_Deal
                 + ArchiveCost_per_AccountMonth
                 + PaymentCost
                 + SupportCost
                 + evaluation_and_security_allocations

GrossMargin = (NetRevenue - COGS) / NetRevenue
```

**Product design implication P-UNIT-01.** Run the model without provider free tiers for steady-state marginal economics, then show free tiers separately as a launch-scale offset. This prevents small-scale credits from masking a structurally uneconomic high-usage cohort.

**Product design implication P-UNIT-02.** Report both gross margin before support and contribution margin after support. The founder's no-high-touch boundary makes support minutes per paying account a core commercial metric, not a footnote.

## 9. Evidence-Backed Implications for Preview, Trial, Refund, and Billing

**Verified fact F-RISK-01.** Current professional-software risk reversal spans: Koyfin's paid-first 30-day satisfaction guarantee; FirmRoom's no-card, fully unlocked 14-day trial except for a ten-user invite cap; Macabacus' no-card 14-day trial; TIKR's 14-day paid-subscriber money-back guarantee; Docusign's 30-day refund for eligible annual web purchases; and Ansarada's free preparation until an external guest is invited or day 90. [S05, S07, S08, S23, S26, S29]

**Evidence-backed inference I-RISK-01.** Trial-first, paid-first refund, and activation-aligned “free until live” mechanisms are all current professional-software patterns. The external evidence does not determine which is safe for confidential uploads, high-cost source processing, or a complete first Deal.

**Product design implication P-RISK-01.** Ticket 10 should decide risk reversal from first-value cost and abuse exposure, not copy a generic free trial. A synthetic public proof can remain free, while confidential upload and material processing can begin after payment with a narrowly defined first-value guarantee or service credit.

**Product design implication P-RISK-02.** Any guarantee must define supported inputs, source rights/authority, minimum source sufficiency, file/size limits, and product-caused failure. It must not promise deal completion, valuation improvement, legal protection, or elimination of professional error.

**Product design implication P-RISK-03.** Koyfin's end-of-period cancellation/access pattern is intelligible for a subscription, while Ticket 6 requires a separate exact boundary for archive export, deletion timing, and account closure. Taxes, refund fee treatment, annual-plan proration, and legal terms require later legal/finance review.

## 11. Research Conclusions for the Separate Ticket 10 Decision

1. **Verified:** Current public self-serve professional pricing spans low horizontal AI/productivity subscriptions, $100–$200 monthly individual AI power-user plans, $200–$360 annual banker add-ins, $209–$349 monthly advisor workflows, and transaction data rooms from hundreds into thousands of dollars per active room-month. The evidence makes a high-ticket self-serve hypothesis credible enough to test; it does not validate a price.
2. **Verified:** Mature products meter understandable commercial objects—seat, data room, storage tier, report volume, or subscription—and expose warnings/limits. Sales-led financial-data and enterprise products generally withhold price or tailor it.
3. **Evidence-backed inference:** The controlled, revisioned Deal outcome is materially broader than a prompt, Office add-in, research terminal, or secure folder. Adjacent low prices are not a defensible price cap.
4. **Verified:** At plausible high-ticket revenue, standard domestic-card processing is a larger directly verified cost than basic OCR and modest object storage. Model use, structured extraction, rendering/validation, retries, support, and security allocations determine the uncertain tail.
5. **Product design implication:** Ticket 10 can safely use active Deals and Deal activation/continuation as visible guardrails while leaving evidence inspection, corrections, citations, reviewer actions, ordinary refresh, and failed-state recovery unmetered. The exact structure remains a Ticket 10 Product Design Decision.
6. **Product design implication:** A calculation-ready unit model should be instrumented around cost per activated Deal, processed source page, Package Revision, active Deal month, support minute, failed/retried job, and archived GB-month, then reprice from observed cohorts rather than token markups.
